# Licensing and Asset Provenance

Rules this project holds itself to, and the current status of everything it
ships.

---

## Hard rules

These are not preferences. A build that violates one of them is a defect.

1. **No ripped game assets.** Nothing from GTA, Forza, Need for Speed, BeamNG,
   commercial driving/flight simulators, or leaked/resold marketplace packs.
2. **No scraped map geometry or imagery.** Nothing extracted from Google Maps,
   Google Earth, Apple Maps, Bing 3D, paid 3D map providers, or street-view
   sources.
3. **No unlicensed facade photography.** Copyrighted photographs of real
   buildings are not shipped as textures.
4. **Branded vehicles only with an explicit licence** that permits *all five*
   of: game use, modification, redistribution, repository inclusion, final
   distribution. If any one is missing, the model does not ship.
5. **No misrepresentation of build state.** The project does not describe
   itself as photoreal or game-ready until it is.

## Audio — why there are no files

Every sound in the build is generated from oscillators and noise buffers in
about 6 KB of JavaScript. Nothing is sampled, nothing is downloaded, and there
is no audio file in the repository to license.

That was a deliberate choice rather than a shortcut. The realistic options for
a city ambience were:

| Option | Problem |
|---|---|
| Commercial sample libraries | Almost all forbid redistribution inside a shipped build |
| "Free" sample packs | Licence terms vary per file and are frequently mis-stated upstream |
| Field recordings of a real street | Carry the rights of whoever recorded them, plus any recognisable voices |
| CC0 collections | Usable, but provenance still has to be verified file by file |

Synthesis has none of those problems and it also does something the samples
could not: the mix is driven by the simulation. The traffic bed is a function
of vehicles actually within 160 m, the crowd murmur of pedestrians within
70 m, the tyre hiss of rain intensity times traffic, and the wind of altitude.

**Verified rather than asserted.** `verify()` in `src/audio.js` renders the
real graph into an `OfflineAudioContext` and measures it. Current results:

| mix | RMS | spectral centroid |
|---|---|---|
| all layers at zero | 0.00000 | – |
| traffic bed only | 0.06478 | 478 Hz |
| rain only | 0.13269 | 2002 Hz |
| siren only | 0.03951 | 3779 Hz |
| horn only | 0.07684 | 2534 Hz |

The traffic bed sits four times lower in the spectrum than the rain, which is
the shape it should have, and silence measures as actual silence.

## OpenStreetMap — ODbL obligations

OSM data is licensed **ODbL 1.0**. Two obligations follow, and both apply to a
shipped game:

**Attribution.** The runtime must display "© OpenStreetMap contributors"
somewhere a player can reach — a credits screen or an about panel is enough.
This is a Phase 2N deliverable and is tracked as a release blocker.

**Share-alike on derived databases.** The registry
(`data/manhattan/buildings/building_registry.csv`) is a *derived database* under
ODbL: it contains OSM ids and OSM-derived geometry. If it is distributed, it
must be distributed under ODbL.

The distinction that matters:

- The *rendered game* — meshes, images, the running application — is a
  **Produced Work**. ODbL does not force the Produced Work to be ODbL. It only
  requires attribution.
- The *registry and caches* — the tabular and geometric data — are the
  **Database**. Distributing those requires ODbL.

Practical consequence: shipping the game is fine under any licence, as long as
attribution is shown. Publishing the registry CSV alongside it puts that CSV
under ODbL.

## NYC Open Data

Published under the NYC Open Data Terms of Use pursuant to Local Law 11 of
2012. Free to use, redistribute and build upon, commercially or otherwise.
Attribution is requested; no warranty is given as to accuracy. NYC's own terms
ask that derived products not imply city endorsement.

The runtime credits line should therefore read something like:

> Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL.
> Building and neighbourhood data from NYC Open Data. Not endorsed by the City
> of New York.

## Vehicle policy (Phase 2F)

Real branded cars are permitted **only** where the specific model's licence
explicitly grants all five rights listed in rule 4. In practice that rules out
almost every free model on Sketchfab and TurboSquid, whose default licences
forbid redistribution inside a game build.

The default plan is therefore **generic procedural vehicles** authored in
Blender: silhouettes typical of NYC traffic — yellow medallion cab, delivery
van, box truck, sedan, SUV, city bus, refuse truck — with no manufacturer
badging, no trademarked grille shapes, and no copied body panels.

Any licensed branded model added later must arrive with:

- the licence text committed to `docs/licences/<model>.txt`
- the five rights explicitly confirmed in that text
- a row in the table above

A model without a committed licence file does not enter the repository.

---

## Audio policy (Phase 2J)

Same standard. Ambient city audio must be either:

- **CC0 / public domain** (e.g. Freesound CC0 uploads), with the source URL and
  uploader recorded in `docs/licences/audio.md`, or
- **procedurally synthesised** at runtime.

No commercial sound library, no game rip, no unlicensed field recording.

## Trademarks and real places

The world is a recognisable model of a real city. That is lawful — buildings in
public view are not protected by copyright in the US, and street layouts are
facts. Two limits apply anyway:

- **No corporate logos or trademarked signage** reproduced on facades. Signage
  is generated with invented brand names.
- **No implied endorsement** by any building owner, brand, or the City of New
  York.

Landmark buildings are represented by their real massing and height, which is
factual data from NYC Open Data. Their surfaces are procedural, not
photographic.

### The one building that is not real

The HQ tower is fiction, and it stands on a real Manhattan lot. That is a
deliberate, single departure from the frozen Phase 1 world and it is recorded
here rather than quietly made:

- **What it replaces.** Exactly one registry building, id 34686, in Midtown
  West. It has no name and no address in any source dataset, and its height is
  modelled from zoning rather than measured (`height_source: zone_model`) — so
  the data does not actually know what stands there. `scripts/phase2/63_hq_site.py`
  chooses the lot by that rule, not by hand, and lists what it covers in
  `data/manhattan/hq/hq_site.json`.
- **What it is not.** Not a model of any real corporate headquarters, in
  Shenzhen or anywhere else. No company's name, mark, livery or facade. The
  design, the massing and every surface colour are generated by
  `scripts/phase2/62_hq.py`.
- **How it is hidden.** The replaced building is suppressed at runtime rather
  than deleted from the tiles, so the exported world data is still the
  unmodified Phase 1 build and the substitution is reversible in one call.

### Attribution is in the build, not just in this file

ODbL 4.3 asks for attribution a reader can find. The runtime shows a legible
notice at all times — it is the one overlay `H` does not hide — and `L` opens a
full panel listing every dataset, its Socrata id and its licence, plus the
statement that this world is a Derivative Database shared under the same
terms. Before Phase 2N that notice named two of the eight datasets and was
drawn at 11px, 65% opacity, with pointer events off. That was technically
present and practically unfindable, which is not what the licence asks for.

### Shenron is an original form, not a character

`SHENRON_form` is a coiling luminous ribbon around a faceted core, with a
handful of suspended motes and four broken halo rings. It has **no face, no
eyes, no scales, no horns and no character features of any kind**. It is not a
reproduction of, and is not derived from, any existing character design, and
nothing in it is traced, measured or copied from one — it is 1,148 triangles
generated by two parametric spirals in `scripts/phase2/64_corridor.py`.

The project's name invites the assumption, so it is worth being explicit: if a
recognisable licensed character is wanted at the end of the corridor, that is
a rights decision to be made and paid for, not a modelling job to be quietly
done here.

The car interior is likewise unbranded — no marque, no badge, no instrument
graphics copied from a real vehicle.

### Mission Control is not fabricated

Floor 45's video wall and all 28 operator monitors are authored **dark**, and
the runtime does not invent telemetry for them. The brief forbids fabricating
Mission Control state while offline, and numbers baked into a vertex colour
would be exactly that: a screenshot of a system reporting figures no system
produced. They stay powered down until something real drives them.
